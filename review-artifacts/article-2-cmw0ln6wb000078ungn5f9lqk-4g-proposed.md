The work is already on your desk. A reply is waiting in the inbox. A booking still needs a time. A post is unwritten. A meeting has finished, and the next steps were never written down. The rest of the inbox is still unsorted. An invoice is overdue. Someone wants a simple report from numbers you already have.

Those are the seven tasks below: a customer email, a set of meeting times, a short social post, meeting notes, an inbox priority list, an invoice message, and a first pass at a report. A tool can prepare each one. A named person still checks the facts and decides what is sent.

[How to Start Using AI in Your Small Business Without Losing Human Control](/blog/how-to-start-using-ai-in-your-small-business-without-losing-human-control) already sets the pattern: one job, one named person, and a way to stop. This article stays with that pattern. It follows what the publishers’ own pages say those tools prepare.

Each illustration below is fictional. It shows the check. It is not a customer story, and it is not a result. A button or a plan on your own account can differ from the help page. If the account in front of you does not show the feature, leave it unused.

## What the tool prepares, and three steps that come after you act

Preparation means the tool drafts, summarises, or proposes. A person sends, publishes, approves, or discards.

A booking stays a proposal until you send the times. A reminder stays unsent until you approve it. You still name the person who may send or publish.

## If the business is in Australia

Read the country, language, and plan limits as part of the task.

Google’s Workspace Privacy Hub, for qualifying Workspace editions, says Gemini interactions stay in the organisation. **Fictional illustration.** A sole trader asks for a short, polite reply explaining that Thursday’s visit has moved to Friday and that the quoted price is unchanged. Before sending, they check the customer’s name, the day, and the price against their own booking note.

**What you check.** Confirm every fact the draft states. Decide whether the tone is acceptable. You are the person who sends it. If Sources shows an unrelated email or file, remove that detail.

**Limits.** If a draft pulls in another email or a Drive file, remove that detail before you send. If the account does not show the draft feature, leave it unused.

**First step.** Draft one reply in the email tool the business already uses. Compare it with the source email before anyone sends it.

A reply often leads to the next question: when can we meet?

The guests are invited by that booking. A booking page from an appointment schedule you already have is an option on that page.

You already chose which times to send. You still decide whether that meeting goes ahead.

Microsoft’s Outlook page says Copilot chat can suggest available times. Copilot can check attendee calendars. Copilot can recommend a mutually convenient time. The page does not state a separate limit on how many people this chat scheduling supports.

No separate headcount is stated here.

**Fictional illustration.** A consultant drafting a reply to two clients clicks Help me schedule, sees one amber slot, drops that slot, and sends the remaining times. The clients’ choice creates the invite.

**What you check.** Check the guests, the duration, and the time zone. Do not send an amber slot as if everyone were free. You send the proposal yourself.

**Limits.** A suggestion can be wrong. The feature also sits behind an eligible plan. If your account does not show it, treat it as unavailable.

**First step.** Propose times for one real meeting, review the colour of each slot, and send only the slots you have checked.

## 3. Social media content preparation

Once the practical arrangements are done, many businesses still need a few public words. A post is a draft until a person publishes it.

**What the tool can prepare.** Google’s prompt help for Gemini in Docs, Sheets, Slides, Vids, and Forms says the samples are illustrative. An eligible Workspace or Google AI plan is required. Google’s Workspace guide, “Use Gemini for marketing,” says you can use the feature. That use is possible only if your organisation supports it. Suggestions may be inaccurate. Suggestions should not be treated as professional advice.

The words are the preparation. You publish them.

**Fictional illustration.** A café owner pastes a short Docs prompt, names this week’s soup and the street address, rejects a draft that adds a discount they are not offering, and posts the checked text themselves.

**What you check.** Check prices, dates, claims, and the call to action. Revise a vague prompt before publication. You publish the post. If the post includes a photorealistic picture, video, or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered, use the platform’s disclosure tool.

**Limits.** A caption can invent an offer.

**First step.** Draft three short posts in a document. Publish none until a person has checked every concrete claim.

## 7. Business reporting

The last task is a question about figures you already keep. Ask it of a clean table, then check the answer yourself.

**What the tool can prepare.** Microsoft’s Excel help says Copilot can answer questions about data in one or more tables, or in ranges laid out like tables. Unstructured data is not supported. Copilot does not modify the workbook. It may return a static table you can insert. It may return a static chart you can insert. Those inserts are not refreshable. Advanced analysis mode on the same page opens a new sheet. Advanced analysis mode can insert refreshable Python.

Google’s Workspace Privacy Hub says “analyzing data in a Google Sheet” is an example of a personalised Gemini response.

This article does not describe Sheets charts or formulas. A detailed Sheets feature page is not part of this evidence.

**Fictional illustration.** An owner with last quarter’s sales in an Excel table asks which service brought in the most revenue, then checks the answer against the total column before mentioning it to anyone else.

**What you check.** Check the answer against the source rows. Ask what period and which columns were used. Keep the sentence out of a bank, tax, or board report until it matches the sheet.

**Limits.** A confident summary can still mis-count. A static chart does not update when the sheet changes. You ask in ordinary language, you review a direct answer, and you can expand the code used to produce it, including an optional summary of that code. You still check the result.

**First step.** Ask one question of a clean table. Compare the answer with a total you calculate yourself.

## One draft, one person, then stop

Choose one of these seven tasks that you already do in a tool your account already shows.

A customer email is a practical place to begin when that draft feature is already there. Take one reply you still owe. Ask for the draft. Leave it unsent. Name the person who may send it, and write their name on the draft. Stop there. That person checks the customer’s name, the day, and any price or promise against your own record. Then they send it, or they discard it.

The same stop fits the other six tasks: a set of meeting times, a short post, meeting notes, the inbox view, an invoice message, or a question about a table. One task. One named person. Nothing is sent or published until that person has checked it.
